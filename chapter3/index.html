The brief

A budget of €274,500

Your company is expanding its premises. Signed contracts require the following payments. You can invest money today in deposits that mature exactly when the payments are due.

All payments occur at year-end. Each deposit's rate is fixed for its entire term. Interest accumulates and is paid at maturity. Deposits are assumed default-free, with no taxes or fees. All figures are fictional. APR means the nominal annual quotation before compounding.

Your decision: Can the company secure every payment with today's budget? Match each payment with an investment of the same maturity.

Put the quotations on the same basis

Complete the missing rates. The first row provides an example. Here, k counts compounding periods per year, not over the whole deposit term.

Maturityk per yearRate per compounding period (%)Effective annual rate (%)

The two-year total return is different from its effective annual rate.

Hint 1 · Start with the compounding period

For an APR quotation, divide APR by k. Monthly compounding means k = 12; semiannual means k = 2.

Hint 2 · Compound, then annualise

EAR = (1 + APR/k)^k − 1
Total return over n years = (1 + EAR)^n − 1

Use decimal rates inside these formulas. For example, 3.60% means 0.036.

Read the yield curve

The chart uses your EAR entries above. A point appears when its entry is numeric. The curve is only correct if those inputs are correct.

Hint · What does each point mean?

Each point is an annualised rate for an investment starting today and ending at that maturity. The two-year rate is not a forecast of the one-year rate in year 2.

Show explanation · Yield curve

The curve slopes upward: 3.00%, approximately 3.66%, then 4.04%. Discount the €90,000 payment using the two-year EAR for two years. A three-year deposit does not mature in time for a payment due after one or two years.

Hint 1 · Match dates before calculating

Use the annualised one-year rate for the first payment, the two-year rate for the second, and the three-year rate for the third.

Hint 2 · Discount each payment separately

Amount invested today for year n = Paymentₙ / (1 + EARₙ)^n

For the two-year deposit, you can equivalently use 90,000 / (1 + 0.036/12)^24. Add the three present values; the funding gap is the total minus €274,500.

04 / 4 · About 5 minutes

Challenge the shortcut

The finance director suggests: “The expansion takes three years. Let's use the three-year effective annual rate for all payments.”

Apply that shortcut to the same payment schedule. Does it change the budget decision?

Discuss your reasoning with your partner and lecturer. Free text is not automatically graded.

Hint · Same annual rate, different exponents

Apply 4.04% to all three payments, but still discount for one, two and three years respectively. Compare the resulting total with €274,500.

Show worked solution · The shortcut

The shortcut overstates the rates applicable to the first two payments, understating their present values. It falsely suggests that the budget is sufficient. Use maturity-matched rates and arrange the missing funding.

Optional extension · About 5 minutes

What changes with an inverted curve?

Keep the payments and budget unchanged, but replace the annual effective rates with 5% for one year, 4% for two years and 3% for three years.

Show extension solution

The shortcut uses rates that are too low for the first two payments, so it overstates their present values. It is inexact for both upward-sloping and inverted curves; the direction of the error changes.
