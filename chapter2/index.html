The brief

Compare like with like

Four buyers offer the following payment schedules for the same business. Ownership transfers today under every offer. There are no further operating obligations. Assume all payments are certain and ignore taxes and transaction costs. The appropriate effective annual discount rate is 8%. Unless stated otherwise, payments arrive at year-end.

A

€100,000
today.

B

€25,000 each year
in years 1–5.

C

€22,000 in year 1;
payments grow by 5% annually, ending in year 5.

D

€15,000 today;
€30,000 each year in years 3–7.

Your task: recommend the offer with the highest present value. First identify when each payment occurs; then decide how to value it.

Hint 1 · Discount each payment

A year-t payment is divided by (1.08)^t. The payment at t = 1 is discounted once.

Hint 2 · Use the annuity shortcut

PV₀ = C × [1 − (1 + r)^(−n)] / r

Here C = 25,000, r = 0.08 and n = 5. The first payment is one period after the valuation date.

Show worked solution · Offer B

Discounted payments: €23,148.15; €21,433.47; €19,845.81; €18,375.75; €17,014.58. Their sum (using unrounded values) is €99,817.75. The annuity formula gives the same result. Adding rounded entries can differ by a few cents.

03 / 4 · About 10 minutes

Handle growth and a delayed start

Offer C · Growing payments

Use the growing-annuity formula to value the five payments. Identify the first cash flow and the number of payments carefully.

Offer D · Delayed payments

First value the five €30,000 payments as an annuity. At what date does that value sit? Bring it to today, then add the immediate payment.

Hint 1 · Choose the valuation date

The standard annuity formula gives a value one period before the first payment. D's first annual payment is at t = 3. The immediate €15,000 is already a value at t = 0.

Hint 2 · Formula and sequence

C: PV₀ = C₁ / (r − g) × [1 − ((1 + g)/(1 + r))^n]

Use C₁ = 22,000, r = 0.08, g = 0.05, n = 5.

D: PV₂ = 30,000 × [1 − 1.08^(−5)] / 0.08
PV₀ = 15,000 + PV₂ / 1.08²

Show worked solution · Offers C & D

C: €96,348.42 today. D: annuity value at t = 2 is €119,781.30; discounted to t = 0 it is €102,693.16. Add €15,000 today to obtain €117,693.16. Discounting the annuity value for three years would move it one year too far.

04 / 4 · About 5 minutes

Make the recommendation

Record your four present values. Select the best offer and calculate its advantage over the next-best offer.

Your explanation is for discussion with your partner and lecturer; it is not automatically graded.

Discussion prompts

Why is comparing undiscounted totals insufficient? How do the amounts and timing of C’s payments differ from B’s? What assumptions make a single 8% rate appropriate here? Would your recommendation necessarily remain valid if payment risk differed across buyers?

Show comparison and model reasoning

D (€117,693.16) > A (€100,000.00) > B (€99,817.75) > C (€96,348.42). D is worth €17,693.16 more today than the next-best offer, A. C totals €121,563.89, which is less than B's €125,000. Both payment amounts and their timing affect value; nominal totals alone cannot determine a present-value ranking. Under the stated assumptions, select D.

Optional extension

What if payments never end?

Buyer E offers €8,000 per year forever, starting at t = 1. Buyer F offers €6,000 at t = 1, growing at 2% per year forever. Use the same 8% rate. Compare their present values with A.

Hint · Perpetuities

For E use C/r. For F use C₁/(r − g); this requires r > g.

Show extension solution

E: 8,000/0.08 = €100,000. F: 6,000/(0.08 − 0.02) = €100,000. Both equal A in present value under the assumptions.
